# What would justify paying for C1?

**Preliminary A2/A3 worksheet · synthetic cases · inconclusive.** The buyer's decision is whether paying for attributable liquidity-seconds saves enough reconciliation work or delivers enough additional service to justify the costs across all payers. No buyer or manager supplied these workloads, prices or benefits. All quantities below are paper inputs or conditional calculations except the three explicitly identified M1 measurements. [Candidate agreement C1, revision bc3d08f](SERVICE-SPEC.md)

The subsequent M6 experiment executed the three onchain arrangements and measured full lifecycle receipts. This worksheet preserves the original assumptions. [Executed comparison and remaining gates](M6-COMPARISON.md)

## Compare the same treasury job

Start each alternative at tick 0 with 2,000 A, 2,000 B, one 300-second period, 0.30% LP fee, spacing 60 and no protocol fee. Seed 100,000,000,000 raw L at [−120, 120). Use six-decimal mock tokens and one initial reference of 1 B/A. Leave 100 B available **in addition** to the inventory. The treasury keeps fees and bears inventory risk. Assume no competing LP liquidity. Give each managed alternative the C1 operation limits, manager, intended actions and fixed recipients. Allow only C1's pool to use its service hook. Score qualifying seconds under C1's definition for every alternative, even when the payment rule ignores that score.

| Arrangement | What the buyer gets and how payment closes | When it could be sufficient or preferable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passive LP with owner exit | Owner seeds the position and can withdraw in kind. No manager or service invoice. The available 100 B stays liquid at the treasury. | A stable unattended range can meet the depth target. There is little to reconcile and no reason to escrow a manager budget. Recovery outside the seed depends on price returning or the owner changing strategy. |
| Bounded manager and simple escrow | Same management and prefunding. Pay elapsed active seconds at 100 B per 300 seconds, regardless of qualification. Early stop revokes authority, reserves accrued pay and releases only the remainder. Owner exit is independent. | Suitable when the buyer wants management availability or already reconciles exceptions cheaply. The manager bears less market-driven pay risk. |
| C1 | The same manager earns for cumulative qualifying seconds. The vault and hook record position transitions, escrow protects unpaid earnings, and treasury exit is separate. | Worth testing if both roles buy the position metric and can identify costly interval disputes or manual checks that these records would remove. |
| Independent observation and programmable settlement | A separate observer reconstructs the **same** predicate and cumulative entitlement from ordered core/vault records, including retired identities. It submits a signed interval statement to escrow. Assume a challenge window and agreed adjudicator. Freeze the service cutoff and revoke the manager on time, reserve disputed funds in escrow, release proven earnings and final unearned funds separately. Owner can exit before the report or resolution. | Can deliver the same payment with no swap callback if parties accept evidence availability, observer trust and settlement delay. Compare reporting, challenges and adjudication with hook cost. A missing report can delay money settlement even when principal is available. |

The observer alternative needs access to complete ordered position and tick transitions, a verifiable opening reference, a final block policy and evidence accepted by the adjudicator. Sampling only end-of-period balances misses an outward-and-return interruption. Interim payments would need certified cumulative reports and protection against double counting. Whether the required records, reporting latency and cost are acceptable is open. Give the retainer and observer the same termination, reserve and payment-independent exit protections in the comparison. Only demonstrated differences count.

## Three paper cases

Times are seconds since activation. Use the same incoming **intentions within each row** across all four arrangements. Outward orders offer B for A with an upper price limit at tick 120. Return orders offer A for B with a lower limit at tick 0. Record actual execution time, input consumed, fill or rejection, ending tick and fees on any later replay. Different liquidity can give different outcomes. The numbers below assume the indicated path and no prior claim. They are settlement arithmetic, not simulated fills.

| Case and incoming intentions | Conditional delivery | Compensation at expiry, whole B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat. Offer up to 1 B at second 90 and up to 1 A at 210. No adjustment. | If both trades stay in the seed, all four deliver 300 qualifying seconds with the manager idle. | Passive 0. Retainer 100. C1 100. Observer 100 after certification. Passive is sufficient for this realized path. |
| Interrupted. Offer up to 700 B at 90, up to 700 A at 210. No adjustment. | If the first reaches tick 120 and the second returns to tick 0, [0, 90) and [210, 300) qualify. Q = 180. A return at 240 instead gives Q = 150. | Passive 0. Retainer 100. C1 and observer each earn 60, leaving 40 refundable. A delayed return gives 50 earned and 50 refundable. |
| Infeasible recovery. Same outward order at 90, no return before expiry. At 120 the three managers each attempt the same rerange to [−240, 240) at 200,000,000,000 L. | At tick 120 the added leg needs about 3,589.09 B gross. The vault has at most 2,700 B including the outward input, and the added leg is capped at 2,000 B. The escrowed 100 B is unavailable. The attempt reverts, the old seed persists, and Q stays 90. Passive makes no attempt. A smaller adjustment would be a different case. | Passive 0. Retainer 100. C1 and observer each earn 30, leaving 70 refundable. The failed operation costs the manager gas and restores no service. |

For the infeasible case the gross B requirement in whole tokens is approximately `200,000 × (1.0001^60 − 1.0001^(−120))`. For all managed alternatives, also compare early termination at second 180 after continuous service and 40 B already paid. Each owes 60 B cumulatively, reserves 20 B and can refund 40 B. Compare who verifies that result, when each transfer clears, and what happens if the manager's receipt fails. The observer may have to establish the amount before releasing funds. No advantage follows from paying out less.

For a later comparison record Q, inventory by token, idle balances, LP fees, amounts paid/reserved/refundable, order shortfall, slippage and marked value at a common declared external price. Separate fees from principal and apply the same marking convention to all arms. A flat path can favor passive LP. An interrupted path can favor the retainer if availability is the job, or the observer if an independently certified statement meets the buyer's evidence needs.

## Provisional cost worksheet

**Horizon and counts.** Use one interrupted period above, expiry at 300, plus one post-expiry 1 A sell intention at 301 with price limit at tick −120, before liquidity removal. Count settlement until claims, refund and principal receipt clear. For each managed arm assume one mandate setup, two acceptances, one funding and one seed activation. Count actual transactions separately where setup, approvals, or calls can be batched. Passive has one seed, no mandate, acceptances or escrow. Counts are proposed workload inputs. Gas per full transaction, actual inclusion times and all customer costs are unknown.

| Payer | Quantities to price per agreement | Missing input, unit and comparison treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treasury | Setup/deployment, one treasury acceptance, funding and activation for managed arms. One owner removal and one receipt in each arm (may be batched). Passive needs only its LP setup and exit. | Measure receipts in gas/operation, including approvals and initialization. Use one row per payer and arrangement in a private evaluation. Enter counts × receipt gas × gas price, hours × evidenced rate, review cost allocation and capital-time cost in a common unit. Keep token quantities separate until a valuation is agreed. Count observer fees as buyer expense for buyer incidence; in the joint total count the underlying resources once. Likewise keep manager compensation and LP fee transfers separate from newly created service or resource savings. Compare payer totals individually even if the treasury does not reimburse traders.

**Measured input and exclusions.** The bounded M1 summary reports incremental execution gas of **97,462 outward**, **57,636 return** and **26,904 after termination**. M1 did not measure complete C1 transaction receipts, acceptance/funding/refund, or closeout under compensation failure. Repeated swaps that leave qualification unchanged are unpriced. Preserve each sample's path and count. Retrieve the archived pins, runners and logs before relying on these figures for engineering or a benchmark. [M1 measurements and limits](M1-STATUS.md)

**Worked hurdle against a no-hook managed alternative.** As a diagnostic only, assign each of the three sampled increments once to the baseline swap workload. Use a synthetic price of 10 gwei/gas and 10⁹ gwei/ETH. The trader component is `(97,462 + 57,636 + 26,904) × 10 / 10⁹ = 0.00182002 ETH`. This prices three specific increments. Applying either active sample to repeated same-state swaps needs new evidence.

| Synthetic sensitivity, other inputs held fixed | Trader increment per period |
| --- | ---: |
| Same three samples at 5 gwei/gas | 0.00091001 ETH |
| Same three samples at 30 gwei/gas | 0.00546006 ETH |
| One outward, one return, 21 ended swaps at 10 gwei/gas, assuming the ended sample recurs each time | 0.00720082 ETH |

Let N be C1's other resource costs minus the alternative's, in ETH per period, including review, management, observer work and capital delay. Let V be the independently valued improvement in delivered service plus reconciliation work avoided. Record saved work in V only if it was excluded from N. For the baseline the joint break-even condition is **V ≥ 0.00182002 ETH + N**. Separately require an acceptable buyer benefit against the treasury's own incremental costs and record whether each affected payer accepts its burden. At an illustrative labor rate of 0.02 ETH/hour and N = 0, the baseline alone would require 5.46006 minutes of avoided work if that were the only benefit. The rate and saving are hypothetical.

**Unfavorable case.** Suppose existing reconciliation already meets the buyer's needs, service delivery is identical, V = 0 and N = 0. C1 then adds the entire 0.00182002 ETH diagnostic trader cost. The interrupted-case 40 B refund reduces treasury spend and manager receipts by the same amount relative to the retainer. It cannot offset this joint cost. Conversely an expensive observer dispute could make N negative. Its frequency, resource cost and effect on time to payment need evidence.

**Decision remains open.** Missing inputs include the actual treasury job and acceptable evidence, one dated reconciliation example, workload by path and payer including traffic after expiry, full comparable receipts, labor and review rates, challenge frequency, capital lock times and each role's cost tolerance. Select the simplest arrangement that meets the confirmed job. Recompute payer and joint hurdles from those inputs before a preliminary A2/A3 decision. Full lifecycle verification remains an M6 task.
